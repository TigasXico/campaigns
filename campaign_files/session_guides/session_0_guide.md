# Session 0 Guide: The World of Aetris & Kythoria

## Overview

This guide covers everything you need for your Session 0. It is divided into:

1. **What to Present** -- World lore players should know (public info only)
2. **The One-Shot Recap** -- What happened, what the party knows, what's at stake
3. **Questions for Players** -- About their characters and expectations
4. **Group Decisions** -- Party composition, critical hits, lore access, and logistics
5. **Session 0 Agenda** -- A step-by-step flow to run the session
6. **Mood-Setting Mechanic** -- Creative system for signaling scene tone

---

## PART 1: WHAT TO PRESENT (Public Knowledge)

### The World at a Glance

> _"The world is called **Aetris**. The campaign takes place on the continent of **Kythoria** -- a vast land of frozen northern tundra, temperate heartlands, subtropical southern coasts, and a western frontier where the war rages on."_

### Quick Summary for Players

> _"It has been **400 years** since the **Abominable End** -- an invasion of endless monsters that poured onto Kythoria's shores from every direction. Six ancient kingdoms fell. The mortal races were pushed to the brink of extinction. **200 years ago**, three capital cities -- **Skywatch**, **Emberforge**, and **Weaveheart** -- were founded as humanity's final stand. From these bastions, the mortal races began to **reconquer** their lost lands. Today, **two-thirds of the continent** has been reclaimed. The south and east thrive. Trade routes hum. But the **north and west** remain contested, and approximately one-quarter of the continent still lies under monster control. Adventurers are needed to push back the darkness, reclaim lost ruins, and forge a future."_

### What Players Know About the World

Here is everything that is **public knowledge** in the world:

#### Geography

- The continent of **Kythoria** spans arctic, temperate, and subtropical zones
- **Three capital cities** form the heart of civilization: Skywatch (military/rangers), Emberforge (industry/forge), Weaveheart (magic/scholarship)
- The **Western Frontier** is where the reconquering is most active -- dotted with military outposts and fledgling settlements
- The **Frozen North** and large parts of the **west** remain under monster control
- Safe **sea routes** connect the eastern and southern coasts
- Other continents exist but are distant and little-known

#### History

- **Era of Foundation** (before 400 years ago): Six mortal kingdoms thrived -- Drakhaven (Humans), Elyndor (Elves), Kazadur (Dwarves), Hearthglen (Halflings), Everquill (Gnomes), Stonefist (Half-Orcs)
- **The Abominable End** (400-200 years ago): Monsters appeared from the coasts, kingdoms fell, ~80% of the population died
- **The Rise of the Mortals** (200 years ago-present): The three capitals were founded, the reconquering began

#### Religion

- There are **10 gods**: Aloria, Thalkis, Kaelthor, Aurelius, Vesper, Crassian, Tyra, Lolth, the Raven Queen, and Vorn
- Each god created a specific mortal race
- **Three gods are publicly blamed** for the monster invasion: Lolth, the Raven Queen, and Vorn
- Their churches are **banned** in Last Stand territory
- The **Ecumenical Council** of the remaining seven gods mediates interfaith matters

#### Society & Governance

- The **Council Accord** (three kings, one per capital) governs
- The **Survival Code** has five articles: Life, Supply, Allegiance, Barrier, Truth
- The **Adventurers' Guild (Drakh Ix'thar)** operates continent-wide -- members called Ix'thari
- Society is militarized -- everyone contributes to the reconquering effort
- Reputation is earned through concrete contributions to reclaiming territory

#### Races

- **Common races**: Humans, Elves, Dwarves, Halflings, Gnomes, Half-Orcs, Half-Elves, Tieflings, Dragonborn
- **Uncommon race**: Genasi (only one)
- **Subraces**: High Elves, Wood Elves, Drow (black-skinned elves of the Underdark), Shadow Elves/Shadar-kai (of the Shadowfell)
- **Racial tensions**: Drow are deeply distrusted (Lolth association). Shadow Elves are distrusted (Raven Queen). Half-Orcs face discrimination (Vorn association). Tieflings are seen as "godless" and face prejudice.

#### Magic

- Magic is real, learned, and practiced
- **Arcane magic**: Studied by wizards, sorcerers, bards
- **Divine magic**: Granted by gods to clerics and paladins
- **Primal magic**: Druids and rangers draw from nature
- **Ki**: Monks discover inner power through discipline

#### Adventuring Context

- **Monster-hunting contracts** issued by the Last Stand army
- **Dungeon delving** into pre-invasion ruins (they contain coins, magic items, treasures)
- **Frontier exploration** into contested or monster-held lands
- **Trade protection** for caravans and ships
- The Adventurers' Guild provides structure, ranks, and support

## PART 3: THE ONE-SHOT RECAP

### How to Run This Section

> **DM note**: Do NOT present this as a monologue. Instead, ask the players to help you recap. Use this structure:
>
> *"We played a two-part one-shot before the campaign. Let's recap together -- I'll start, and you fill in the gaps."*
>
> Then go through each section below and **ask the players** what they remember, what their characters did, and what stood out. This validates their experience, surfaces details you may have forgotten, and gets everyone back into character.

### The Setup -- How It All Started

> Ask the players: *"Who hired us? What do we know about them?"*

Before the campaign proper begins, the party (minus Aegon) was assembled by a shadowy mob boss known as **FX**. He met with the party **directly** -- a controlled meeting in a secure location. Charismatic. Calculating. Dangerous beneath the surface. He offered a lucrative contract:

> *"House Thormvuld has built a floating estate called Seidga'ard. They found something -- crystals, a new kind of magic. I want to know what they're hiding in that island. I want samples. I want schematics. And I'll pay handsomely for all of it."*

**Support provided**: FX gave the party Aegis Tokens (to bypass the anti-magic grid), Construct IFF Tags (to avoid automated defenses), and a Sending Box/Receiving Box system to smuggle their gear past security. He also had an inside man -- **Ka'el** -- embedded as a servant in Seidga'ard.

### Phase 1 -- Intel Gathering

> Ask the players in the intel party: *"What was your approach? What did you find?"*

**Party**: Zan, Aurora, Bardolemeu (Sanzo was present but is now unavailable)

Their mission: infiltrate **Seidga'ard** disguised as **catering staff** setting up for the grand gala. Map defenses, gather intelligence on the crystal research, and find out what House Thormvuld was hiding.

**Key discovery**: The intel party discovered the existence of a **underground secret research facility** beneath Seidga'ard -- hidden labs running experiments with unidentified magical crystals. *(FX recognized the experimental methods as something he had seen before. He hasn't shared what he knows with the party.)*

> *[Ask players: "Anything else you remember from the infiltration? Any close calls?"]*

### Phase 2 -- The Extraction

> Ask the extraction party: *"Walk us through the heist. How did it go down?"*

**Party**: Odessa, Zan (as liaison between both teams), Kaelyn, Balor

Their cover: **diplomatic ambassadors of the Dark Alliance** -- a recently forged political alliance between the **Drow** and **Shadow Elves** (the two races Kaelyn and Zan belong to). This cover got them past the initial security checkpoint at Seidga'ard's grand gala.

Their mission: reach the underground facility and the **Core Cave** (the ancient source cavern beneath the island), extract crystal samples, Tuning Cylinders, and the levitation engine schematics.

> *[Ask players: "How did the heist play out? Any moments you're proud of? Anyone almost die?"]*

**Optional vault heist**: The party also had the opportunity to crack the Thormvuld family vault, recovering additional items: a Stealth-Weave Jacket, the Vendetta Dirk, a Fulgurite Bloodwell Vial, and the Mantle of the Fallen Chieftain.

### What Was Stolen

The heist was a success. The party escaped with:
1. **Solid and liquid crystal samples** (unidentified magical crystals with a primal, never-before-seen arcane signature)
2. **Complete schematics for the levitation engine**
3. **Prototype Tuning Cylinders** (their exact function is unknown to the party -- they appear to channel the crystals' energy somehow)
4. **Vault items** (optional: Stealth-Weave Jacket, Vendetta Dirk, Fulgurite Bloodwell Vial, Mantle of the Fallen Chieftain)

### What the Party Knows About FX

> Ask the players: *"What do we know about FX as a person?"*

- FX met them **directly** -- charming, well-dressed, sharp, dangerous
- He provided specialized gear for the heist (Aegis Tokens, IFF Tags, Sending Box)
- He had an inside man named **Ka'el** embedded in House Thormvuld
- He said the job was commissioned by a **rival merchant guild from another continent**
- He took all the stolen materials and arranged to sell the art and relics to pay the party later
- He seemed unusually interested in the underground lab -- more than the crystals themselves
- He's well-connected, dangerous, and worth keeping as an ally (or a safe distance from)

### The Consequences (Present State)

**The party is being hunted by House Thormvuld:**

| Hunter | Why? | How? |
|--------|------|------|
| **House Thormvuld** | Stolen schematics = collapse of their technological monopoly. Stolen crystals = competitive advantage evaporates. The underground lab's secrets are exposed. | Private investigators, bounty hunters, informants across the continent. They want the schematics back **quietly** -- no Council Accord involvement. The party doesn't know who else may be watching. |

## PART 4: QUESTIONS FOR PLAYERS (Character Questions)

### For Each Player (Individual)

Go around the table and ask each player these questions about their character:

#### Core Questions

1. **"What is your character's name, race, and class?"** (Confirm choices)
2. **"Why is your character an adventurer?"** (Motivation)
3. **"What is your character's relationship with the gods?"** (Devout? Skeptical? Indifferent?)
4. **"What does your character think about the war and the monsters?"** (Fear? Hatred? Duty? Curiosity?)
5. **"Where does your character call home? Do they have family or friends?"**
6. **"Does your character have any short-term goals? Long-term dreams?"**
7. **"What is your character's biggest flaw?"** (For RP hooks)
8. **"What would make your character abandon the party?"** (Avoid party conflict)

#### Post-Heist Specifics

Ask each player who participated in the one-shot:

9. **"How does your character feel about having stolen from the Thormvulds?"**
10. **"What did your character do with their share of the payment?"**
11. **"Does your character trust FX? Fear FX? Want to work with FX again?"**
12. **"Since the heist, have you noticed anyone following you? Strange questions from strangers?"**

#### For Specific PCs

**Aegon's player:**
- Aegon did NOT participate in the heist. How do you want him to meet the group? Options:
  - A) He was hired by the party afterward when they realized they needed muscle
  - B) He was investigating the Thormvulds independently and crossed paths
  - C) His visions led him to the party (they are all B-touched -- he sensed them)
  - D) He was sent by the Order of the Scales to investigate rumors about the heist
- How does Aegon feel about being called "The Rogue Paragon"? Does he embrace or resent the title?
- Has he told anyone about his shifting visions? If so, who, and what did they say?

**Aurora's player:**
- How long has Aurora been away from Elarwyn? Does she plan to return?
- During the heist, did the crystals resonate with her hidden constellations?

**Balor's player:**

## PART 5: GROUP DECISIONS

### Session 0 Checklist -- Let the Group Decide

#### Decision 1: Party Structure

> **How do we organize the group going forward?**

Present the options clearly:

- **Option A: One Big Party** -- All 7 players (minus Sanzo) play together in every session. Pros: everyone together, shared story. Cons: scheduling 7 people is hard, spotlight time is limited, combat bogs down.

- **Option B: Two Smaller Groups** -- Split into 2 groups based on character goals, player availability, or preferred playstyle. Each group plays separate sessions in the same world, with potential crossovers. Pros: easier scheduling, more spotlight per player, deeper character moments. Cons: separate stories to track, less party interaction.

- **Option C: Three Smaller Groups** -- Split into 3 groups of 2-3 players. Same pros/cons as Option B, but even more flexible scheduling.

**Suggested splits to discuss:**

| Group | Characters | Theme |
|-------|-----------|-------|
| **The Crystal Resonators** | Aegon, Aurora, Kaelyn, Zan | Cosmic mystery, strange connection to the crystals, divine conspiracy |
| **The Hunted** | Balor, Nyra, Odessa | Revenge quests, personal vendettas, searching for lost family |
| **The Wild Card** | Bardomeleu (fits either group as social/face) | Investigation, underworld connections, social intrigue |

**Key question for players**: What is your availability like? How often can you commit to playing?

#### Decision 2: Critical Hit Mechanic

> **How do we handle critical hits?**

The group is currently using a home rule: **Max dice + roll** (e.g., a 2d6 weapon crit does 12 + 2d6). Present the options:

| Option | How It Works | Pros | Cons |
|--------|-------------|------|------|
| **A) Current Home Rule** | Max damage + rolled damage (12 + 2d6) | Guaranteed high damage, crits always feel impactful | Very high damage potential, deadly for both sides |
| **B) Double Dice (Traditional 5e)** | Roll double the dice (4d6 instead of 2d6) | RAW, balanced, random | Can feel underwhelming if you roll low (two 1s) |
| **C) Double the Result** | Roll normally, double the total (2d6 = X, then x2) | Simple, consistent | Same randomness issue, can still feel weak |

> **Important**: Whichever rule the party picks, **enemies will use the same rule**. If you want deadly crits for both sides, pick A. If you want balanced randomness, pick B or C.

#### Decision 3: Lore Access via World Anvil

> **How do we share lore after sessions?**

The lore content will be made available via **World Anvil**. Access follows the **rule of demand**: players take notes during sessions, and after each session, each player DMs the GM with what topics they want lore access to. Individual character rolls and backstories may limit access.

Present two options:

| Option | How It Works | Pros | Cons |
|--------|-------------|------|------|
| **A) Individual Access** | Each player gets access only to the topics THEY requested. Different players may have different lore knowledge. | Respects backstory privacy, rewards note-taking, realistic knowledge gaps | Harder to track, party may have conflicting info |
| **B) Shared Party Access** | All topics requested by any player are accessible to the whole party. | Simple to manage, everyone stays on same page | May spoil discoveries meant for specific characters, less incentive for individual note-taking |

> **GM note**: Regardless of the option chosen, **backstory privacy is always respected**. Each character's personal backstory files are only shared at the player's discretion. The decision is about WORLD lore access (public knowledge, organizations, locations, etc.).

#### Decision 4: Campaign Tone

> **What atmosphere are we creating together?**

- **Core tone**: Serious -- mystery, political intrigue, drama, emotional discoveries
- **But**: There will ALWAYS be space for **goofiness, flirtation, and silliness**

The group should agree on boundaries and comfort levels for:
- How dark is too dark? (Torture, child harm, sexual violence, etc.)
- How silly is too silly? (Does goofy RP break immersion for anyone?)
- Any specific topics anyone wants as hard **lines** (don't go there) or **veils** (fade to black)?

## PART 6: SESSION 0 AGENDA (Step-by-Step)

> Run the session in this order. Estimated time: 3-4 hours.

### Step 1: Welcome & Introduction (15 min)

- Thank everyone for coming
- Explain what Session 0 is for: set expectations, make group decisions, recap the one-shot, plan the campaign
- Share the **elevator pitch** (from Part 1)

### Step 2: World Presentation (30-45 min)

Present the public lore from Part 1:

1. **The Map of Kythoria** (describe geography, regions, climate)
2. **The Three Eras** (Foundation, Abominable End, Rise of the Mortals)
3. **The Three Capitals** (Skywatch, Emberforge, Weaveheart -- their cultures)
4. **The Gods & Religion** (10 gods, 3 banned, the Ecumenical Council)
5. **The Races** (which exist, their homelands, social tensions)
6. **Society** (Council Accord, Survival Code, Adventurers' Guild)
7. **What Adventurers Do** (monster hunting, dungeon delving, frontier exploration)

### Step 3: The One-Shot Recap (20 min)

Recap what happened (from Part 3):

1. FX hired the party for a heist on House Thormvuld's floating estate **Seidga'ard**
2. Two sub-parties: Intel (Zan, Aurora, Bardolemeu -- catering cover) and Extraction (Odessa, Zan, Kaelyn, Balor -- Dark Alliance ambassador cover)
3. What was stolen (magical crystal samples, levitation engine schematics, Tuning Cylinders, vault items)
4. **Current situation**: The party is being hunted by House Thormvuld. They have dangerous cargo. They need to figure out what to do next.
5. **Aegon's introduction**: Aegon was not in the heist. How does he join the party? (Discuss options from Part 4.)

### Step 4: Character Introductions (30-45 min)

Go around the table. Each player introduces their character:

- **"Who are you?"** (Name, race, class)
- **"Where are you from?"** (Location, family)
- **"Why are you here?"** (Motivation for adventuring)
- **"How did the heist affect you?"** (For those who participated)
- **"What is one thing you want everyone at the table to know about your character?"**
- **"What is one thing your character keeps secret?"** (Player shares with you, not the group)

### Step 5: Group Decisions (45-60 min)

Work through the checklist from Part 5:

1. **Party structure**: One big party, or split into groups? (Biggest decision of the session)
2. **Critical hit mechanic**: Keep the home rule, or switch?
3. **Lore access**: Individual or shared via World Anvil?
4. **Campaign tone**: Set lines, veils, and boundaries
5. **Mood-setting mechanic**: Choose and agree how to use it
6. **Starting location**: Where after the heist?
7. **Map**: Set expectations (not ready yet)
8. **Scheduling**: Date/time/frequency for Session 1 and beyond

### Step 6: Individual Player Questions (15-30 min)

For players with open backstory questions, use this time to fill in the blanks. Ask the questions from Part 4.

### Step 7: The First Hook (15 min)

Close with a taste of what comes next. Read this aloud:

> *"The heist is over. The payday is spent or stashed. You've been lying low in **Crossroads** -- a frontier settlement where questions are few and coins spend well. But this morning, something changed.*
>
> *A message arrives, delivered by a street urchin who vanishes into the crowd before anyone can ask questions. The parchment is plain. The handwriting is careful. It reads:*
>
> *'You have something the Thormvulds want. They have something I want. We can help each other. Sunset. The Rusty Compass. Come alone, or don't come at all. -- A Friend'*
>

## PART 7: MOOD-SETTING MECHANIC

### The Problem

The campaign's core tone is serious -- mystery, political intrigue, drama, emotional weight. But there must always be room for **goofiness, flirtation, and silliness**. The challenge: signaling when we're in a dramatic scene vs. when we're in a lighthearted one, so no player feels like they're "breaking the mood" by being silly, or "ruining the fun" by being serious.

### Option A: The Mood Stones (Full System)

A tactile system using colored stones at the table. It has **two zones**: one for the GM, one shared by the players.

#### The Layout

```
        [GM's Stone Zone]     <-- sets the scene's baseline mood
         _________________
        |  o  o  o  o  o  |    <-- each player's personal stones
        |  o  o  o  o  o  |    <-- placed in front of each player
        |  [ CENTER ZONE  |    <-- shared area: players can place stones here
        |_  for group mood]_|
```

- **GM's zone** (near the DM): The GM places one stone here to set the **baseline mood** of the current scene
- **Player's personal stones** (in front of each player): Each player has their own set of colored stones. They can place one in front of themselves to signal their personal mood/intent for their current action
- **Center zone** (middle of table): If multiple players place the same color stone forward, the table sees a consensus mood shift

#### The Colors

| Color | Mood | Description |
|-------|------|-------------|
| **Blue** | Mystery / Discovery | Investigation, lore drops, revelations |
| **Red** | Danger / Combat | Tension, threat, initiative imminent |
| **Purple** | Emotion / Drama | Backstory reveals, heavy character moments |
| **Gold** | Light / Fun / Goofy | Jokes welcome, silliness encouraged, flirtation tolerated |
| **Green** | Exploration / Travel | Journey, world-building, discovering places |
| **White** | Neutral / Social RP | General roleplay. Can go any direction. |

#### How to Use It

1. **GM sets the scene**: "You enter the Thormvuld family's private archive..." -- GM places a **blue** stone in their zone. The table knows: this is a mystery/investigation scene.

2. **Player signals intent**: Bardomeleu's player wants to flirt with a guard mid-investigation. They place their **gold** stone forward and say: *"I lean against the doorframe and try my luck."* The table knows: this is a fun moment, not breaking the scene.

3. **Consensus shift**: Multiple players place **purple** stones forward during a tense revelation. The GM can acknowledge the tone shift.

4. **GM overrides**: Combat breaks out -- GM replaces their blue stone with a **red** one. Tone changes.

#### Why This Works

- **Two-way communication**: GM signals mood TO players; players signal mood TO GM
- **Individual agency**: Each player can have their own tone without derailing the scene
- **No interrupting**: Players signal intent non-verbally before speaking
- **Permission structure**: Everyone knows when it's okay to be silly vs. when to lock in
- **Low overhead**: Just colored stones

### Option B: The Binary System (Simplified)

If the full system feels like too much overhead, here's a stripped-down version:

#### Two Zones, Two Colors

Each player has **two tokens** (or a two-sided token):
- **Black side (facing up)** = "I'm in serious mode -- heavy story, emotional beats, pay attention"
- **White side (facing up)** = "Light mode -- jokes, silliness, chill vibes, feel free to relax"

Additionally, the **GM has a center token** that signals the scene's baseline:
- **Black** = "This scene is serious. Drama, tension, mystery."
- **White** = "This scene is light. Relaxed RP, goofing around encouraged."
- **No token** = "Neutral. Go wherever feels right."

#### How It Works

1. GM places their center token before describing a scene
2. Each player flips their personal token to match their mood
3. Mismatch is fine -- it tells the table that one character is being serious while everyone else is goofing (great RP potential)
4. Players can flip mid-scene

#### Why This Works

- **Ultra-simple**: Two colors, one decision
- **Individual expression**: Each player's token shows their personal headspace
- **Clear signal**: Black = "listen up, this matters to my character" / White = "I'm here to have fun"
- **No colors to memorize**: Just serious vs. light

### Which to Choose?

| System | Best For |
|--------|----------|
| **Option A (Mood Stones)** | Groups that want nuanced, scene-by-scene tone signaling with lots of texture |
| **Option B (Binary)** | Groups that want a simple "pay attention vs. chill" signal without managing multiple colors |

> **The group should decide together in Session 0.**

### Alternative: Scene Cards (Non-Stone Version)

If physical stones aren't available, use small cards with symbols:
- **Raven** = Mystery / Intrigue
- **Crossed swords** = Combat / Danger
- **Drama mask** = Emotion / Drama
- **Tankard** = Fun / Goofy
- **Compass** = Exploration

Each player gets a deck of 5 cards and places the relevant one face-up.

### Alternative: The Tone Die

A large custom d6/d8 where each face has a symbol. The GM rolls it (or places it with the chosen face up) at the start of each scene. Simpler than stones but less player agency.
