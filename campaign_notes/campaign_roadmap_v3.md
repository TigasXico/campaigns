# PART 0: DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

This campaign is built on **five pillars**:

### 1. Sandbox with limited options
3-4 active quests at all times. The party chooses which to pursue. The world reacts to their choices. Quests escalate, expire, or resolve based on what the party does and doesn't do.

### 2. Conspiracy layer cake
The truth is buried in layers. Each arc peels back one or two, but never all at once. Lore beats from the same conspiracy topic are **distributed and interleaved** — no two back-to-back. The party assembles the picture piece by piece, not exposition dump by exposition dump.

### 3. PC arcs are the main plot
Every player character's backstory connects directly to one or more conspiracy layers. PC arcs are not side quests — they *are* the path through the campaign. The party discovers the world's secrets by discovering each other's.

### 4. Living war
The monster army is active and responsive. Travel is dangerous. The frontier shifts. The monster hoarding escalation and wave timing depend on **player actions**, not a fixed script. The party can accelerate, delay, redirect, or trigger entirely new outcomes.

### 5. The B-Crystal influence escalates
B-Crystals are not static threats. As the campaign progresses and more deposits are unearthed, ambient resonance in the Weave grows stronger. This manifests in two parallel tracks:

**Monster side**: Hoarding behavior becomes more intense and aggressive. The monster army gains momentum — small packs become coordinated raiding parties, raiding parties become siege forces, siege forces become capital-scale invasions. The monsters' goal shifts from mindless destruction to **plundering**: gather as much CMGMI as possible.

**Mortal side**: Progressive infection creates fractures within mortal society. Frontier tension becomes town-level conflict becomes settlement infighting becomes city-wide paranoia. If left unchecked, the influence reaches a point where no mortal institution can be trusted — everyone becomes a potential vector.

# PART 1: THE B-CRYSTAL INFLUENCE SYSTEM

This system runs in the background of the entire campaign. The influence grows and evolves based on party actions, world events, and the passage of game time. It is not a script — it is a pressure that the party can accelerate, slow, or redirect.

---

### 1.1 Monster Hoarding Escalation

| Stage | Behavior | Examples for Improv |
|-------|----------|---------------------|
| **1. Scattered hoards** | Individual monsters or small packs guard treasure caches. Random finds for adventurers. | A pack of crystal wolves drags shiny rocks back to its den. A lone ettercap hoards gems in a hollow tree. A wyvern's nest is lined with copper pieces. |
| **2. Coordinated raids** | Monster groups attack settlements specifically to steal CMGMI. Frontier villages under pressure. | A goblin warband hits a mining outpost, ignores the workers, makes off with the payroll. A manticore tears the roof off a general store to get at the lockbox. |
| **3. Settlement sieges** | Organized monster forces target towns and trade routes. The Last Stand army mobilizes. | A hobgoblin legion lays siege to a frontier town, demanding tribute in gold. A dragon demands a kingdom's treasury or it will burn the capital. |
| **4. Capital-scale invasion** | A massive, hoarding-driven army push targets one or more Last Stand capitals. | A coordinated assault on Skywatch, Emberforge, or Weaveheart — the party must choose which to defend. The monster army's commanders are themselves deeply infected. |

### 1.2 Mortal Infection Progression

| Stage | Behavior | Examples for Improv |
|-------|----------|---------------------|
| **1. Subtle** | Frontier settlers, miners, adventurers with large personal hoards develop mild attachment to their possessions. | A miner refuses to sell a particularly pretty geode. A farmer hides his savings under the floorboards and grows paranoid. A merchant hoards gold instead of reinvesting. |
| **2. Noticeable** | Town officials, local crime bosses, wealthy merchants prioritize wealth accumulation over duty. | A mayor redirects relief funds into his personal vault. A crime boss's thugs carry crystal-tipped weapons. A merchant guild hoards trade goods, causing shortages. |
| **3. Significant** | Military captains, city council members, powerful guild leaders are compromised. Trust erodes. | A city guard captain hoards the armory's best weapons. A council member votes against spending coin on public defense. Internal investigations are stonewalled by infected officials. |
| **4. Acute** | Capital kings, high clergy, the Council Accord itself are under influence. No mortal institution is trustworthy. | A king's treasury grows while the army goes unpaid. The Ecumenical Council demands tithes in gemstones. Infected leaders begin infighting — cities turn on each other. |

### 1.3 How Stages Progress

The influence advances through **two converging pressures**:

1. **Party actions & discovery** — Every time the party (or a rival faction like the Covenant or Thormvulds) brings a new B-crystal deposit to the surface or into circulation, ambient resonance in the Weave increases globally. One small cache might nudge the needle; a major vein can push an entire stage forward.

2. **Time & inaction** — If no one actively contains deposits or purges infected areas, the influence compounds. Monsters continue hoarding. Mortals continue accumulating. The self-feeding cycle strengthens.

### 1.4 Counter-Pressure Mechanisms

The party can slow or reverse progression locally and globally through the following actions. Each has different costs, risks, and effectiveness depending on the stage of influence they face.

| # | Mechanism | What It Does | Cost / Risk | Scope |
|---|-----------|-------------|-------------|-------|
| 1 | **Destroy B-crystals** | Physically destroying a B-crystal deposit removes its ambient resonance from the Weave. The influence weakens in that region. | Dangerous — crystals can discharge violently when destroyed (wild magic zones, explosions). May attract attention from the Covenant or Thormvulds. | **Local — immediate** |
| 2 | **Clear monster nests / hoards** | Breaking up monster encampments and scattering their CMGMI hoards removes concentrated sources of influence. Combat-driven, classic adventuring. | Combat risk. Hoards must be truly scattered — merely stealing the treasure and keeping it concentrated elsewhere just moves the problem. | **Local — immediate** |
| 3 | **Wealth redistribution** | Breaking up large CMGMI concentrations by distributing them across many people and holdings. Counter-intuitive — the party actively gives treasure away. | Requires a trustworthy institution to distribute through. Hoarders may fight back. The party forfeits monetary reward. | **Local — immediate** |
| 4 | **B-Crystal research (long-term countermeasure)** | Studying the crystals to develop a lasting magical effect — a ward, an enchantment, or a ritual that filters B's subconscious echo from the Weave in a region. | Requires rare components, high-level arcane knowledge, dedicated research time, and a facility (Weaveheart is ideal). Draws attention from Covenant and gods' agents. | **Regional — permanent once developed** |
| 5 | **Covenant-derived countermeasures** | The Covenant has centuries of B-crystal research. They may already have containment fields, tuned containers, or resonance-dampening technology. Steal, trade for, or reverse-engineer. | Requires engaging with the Covenant — risky. Acquired countermeasures may be incomplete, booby-trapped, or require ongoing maintenance. | **Variable** |
| 6 | **Rite parallel / monastic cleansing** | Meditative practices (like Balor's Rite of Ash, or Sanzo's monastic ki) can quiet B's influence on individuals. Monasteries become sanctuaries where infected can recover. | Requires time and isolation. Can't be mass-deployed easily. Monasteries may not trust outsiders. Recovery takes extended periods. | **Individual — slow** |
| 7 | **B-Touched as lightning rods** | The four B-touched PCs (Zan, Kaelyn, Aurora, Aegon) naturally resonate with B's essence. They could draw influence away from a region into themselves — acting as living anchors. | **Extremely dangerous** — accelerates their personal infection. Could cause addiction, loss of control, or permanent modification. High party-drama potential. | **Local / regional — high risk** |
| 8 | **Containment vaults** | Physically sealing B-crystal deposits underground or in anti-magic vaults prevents ambient resonance from escaping. Doesn't destroy — contains. | Requires engineering and resources. A vault can be breached. The crystals remain dangerous if rediscovered or unearthed later. | **Local — temporary containment** |

# PART 2: THE CONSPIRACY LAYERS

The campaign's secrets are organized into **four core layers + one supporting thread**. Each layer answers a different question about the world. They are designed to be discovered in overlapping, interleaved fashion — the party assembles the whole picture across multiple arcs, with PC backstories serving as the primary entry points.

---

### 2.1 Layer 1 — The Crimson Covenant

**Core question**: *Who is hunting us and why?*

**What this layer reveals**: A secret organization of dissident scholars, priests, and arcanists operates across Kythoria. They experiment with B-crystals, hunt specific individuals, and work toward resurrecting an ancient entity. Their reach extends from hidden frontier facilities to the highest levels of mortal society.

**Revelation schedule** (embedded, no fixed session numbers — triggered by party actions and arc progression):

| Reveal | How the Party Learns It |
|--------|------------------------|
| The Flesh Architects symbol belongs to an organization that experimented on living subjects | Gamma-7 records — first concrete evidence |
| Zan was Subject 242 — his file exists at Gamma-7 | Gamma-7 records + Zan's recognition. Personal connection to the facility. |
| The crystals are reactive to living tissue — they *merge* | Encounter with the live B-crystal sample at Gamma-7. Visceral, not intellectual. |
| House Thormvuld copied this organization's research | Thormvuld agents at Gamma-7 + documents. Establishes the Thormvulds are *followers*, not originators. |
| A larger organization exists behind the Flesh Architects — the Crimson Covenant | Covenant agent encounter, or deeper Gamma-7 records. First mention of the name. |
| The Thormvuld experiments are a replication of Flesh Architects' work | Documents at Gamma-7 + Thormvuld agents' orders. Connects two threads. |
| The Crimson Covenant is real, active, and ancient | Intercepted communication, captured agent, or found records. Name and existence confirmed. |
| The Covenant wants to resurrect something using the crystals | Interrogated agent, or Covenant researcher's journal. "Something" — not yet named. |
| Balor is the Covenant's primary target (Project Genesis) | A Covenant agent recognizes him at sight, or Balor finds his own file. Personal stakes. |
| Zan is also wanted (escaped Subject 242) | Same source as above. Parallel reveal for Zan. |
| The Covenant has a network of hidden facilities across the frontier | Map or records from Gamma-7 deeper levels. Opens exploration possibilities. |
| The Covenant is not a monolith — it has research divisions | Covenant structure document found. Contextualizes the Flesh Architects. |
| The Romeo family has ties to the Covenant (intelligence network) | Bardolemeu's investigation, or Midnight Troupe encounter. Bardolemeu's arc trigger. |
| The Covenant's ultimate goal: resurrect B to destroy the gods | Confrontation with a Cardinal-level Covenant member. Big Layer 2 climax. |

### 2.2 Layer 2 — B-Crystals & Entity B

**Core question**: *What are these crystals? What is B?*

**What this layer reveals**: The crystals are fragments of a sacrificed primordial being named B. B gave mortals magic by shattering its own form. The hoarding instinct is B's lingering subconscious will — a desire to become whole again. The Weave itself is B's scattered body.

**Revelation schedule**:

| Reveal | How the Party Learns It |
|--------|------------------------|
| The crystals cause hoarding behavior in monsters | Observed repeatedly across multiple encounters. Confirmed pattern. |
| The crystals predate the gods | Geological analysis, druidic lore (Aurora), or ancient site. Simple age fact. |
| This hoarding is B's subconscious will — a desire to reunite | Aurora's fully-deciphered constellations, or deep Covenant research. Conceptual leap. |
| The crystals are fragments of a sacrificed primordial entity | Ancient site inscriptions + Covenant research cross-referenced. The nature of B. |
| Currency metals are also fragments of this entity | Weaveheart scholar (Asterion?) or Aegon's visions. Economic implications — every coin is a piece of a dead god. |
| The entity is called "B" | Deep Covenant records or ancient source. Name revealed. |
| The Weave itself is B's lingering body | Weaveheart research — the Weave can be *read*. Cosmic scale revealed. |
| B sacrificed itself intentionally to give mortals magic | Same source as above, or direct communication with B's consciousness. B's *choice* — not an accident. |

### 2.3 Layer 3 — The Divine Conspiracy

**Core question**: *Who really caused the monster invasion?*

**What this layer reveals**: The monster invasion was not a natural disaster or a punishment from the gods. It was a planned genocide. The gods orchestrated it via planar pacts. All 10 knew and approved. The three "banned" gods (Lolth, Raven Queen, Vorn) are scapegoats — they secretly coordinate with the other seven. The Covenant holds a blackmail artifact that keeps this cold war in check.

**Revelation schedule**:

| Reveal | How the Party Learns It |
|--------|------------------------|
| The monster invasion was *planned* — not a natural disaster | Evidence at a sealed breach site, or recovered historical records that contradict the public story. First crack. |
| Someone organized the invasion — arcanists are being silenced for investigating | Aegon's visions show a scholar killed by the Order. Or the party finds a murdered researcher. The silencing is ongoing. |
| The gods orchestrated the invasion via planar pacts | Ancient records, a captured divine agent, or the Covenant's own documentation. The big reveal. |
| All 10 gods knew and approved | Same source as above. The "banned three" are not the villains. |
| The three "banned gods" are scapegoats | Same source as above. Political/theological earthquake. |
| B sacrificed itself *because* A and C wanted to destroy mortals | Deepest lore — Weaveheart archives, Underdark texts, or Shadowfell records. B's sacrifice was reactive, not proactive. |
| The Covenant holds a blackmail artifact — broadcasts the truth to all mortals if not reset daily | Covenant inner circle reveal. Explains why the gods haven't simply destroyed the Covenant. The cold war. |

### 2.4 Layer 4 — The False Firmament

**Core question**: *Are the gods even real gods?*

**What this layer reveals**: The entire spiritual foundation of the mortal world is a fabrication. The creation myth is false. Divine magic comes from the mortal caster, not the god. The pantheon's holy wars are staged theater. The gods are not original beings — they are fragments of A and C. Faith is a harvesting mechanism that strengthens them.

**Revelation schedule**:

| Reveal | How the Party Learns It |
|--------|------------------------|
| The creation myth is a fabrication — the gods did not create the world or mortal races | Cross-referencing Covenant records with ancient pre-god sites. Foundation of religious authority is a lie. |
| The pantheon's rivalries, schisms, and holy wars are staged theater to deepen faith | Evidence that "enemy" churches coordinate. The "banned three" secretly work with the other seven. All designed to keep mortals divided and believing. |
| Faith is a harvesting mechanism — worship strengthens the gods | Deep research into the nature of divine power. The gods have a direct, selfish interest in controlling belief. |
| "Divine magic" is the mortal manipulating the Weave — the god only adds their signature, claiming credit | Magic is the manipulation of arcane energy (the Weave), which exists everywhere and within every living being. The mortal's inner spark is the source; the Weave is the flame; components are fuel. The god adds their arcane signature to make it *feel* divine. The mortal already had the power. |
| Ki is the ability to harness the inner arcane spark without external components — monasteries know Ki comes from within, not from any god | Sanzo's arc, or a monastery visit. Independent evidence that the divine magic system is a lie, accessible without gods. |
| The gods are fragments of A and C — not true creators, not original | Deepest lore. The gods are themselves *created beings*, subdivided from the two Originals who wanted genocide. They are not the top of the hierarchy. |

### 2.5 Supporting Thread — The True Origin of Mortal Races

**Core revelation**: Drow, Shadow Elves (Shadar-kai), and Tieflings were not created by the gods. They are unintended byproducts of the Crimson Covenant's Project Genesis — experiments using Khar'zul's demonic essence grafted onto mortal captives. Elven leadership knew and surrendered their own people to the Covenant.

**Revelation schedule**:

| Reveal | How the Party Learns It |
|--------|------------------------|
| Drow, Shadow Elves, and Tieflings were created by the Covenant, not gods | Kaelyn's Underdark archives or deep Covenant records. Origin of races conspiracy. |
| Elven leadership surrendered captives to the Covenant for experiments | Eryx's proof (Odessa's arc). A war crime revealed centuries later. |
